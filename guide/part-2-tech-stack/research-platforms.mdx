At Inflection, one partner was researching European defense while another was looking at counter-UAV technology. Their notes lived in Notion, Signal threads, and personal documents. They were doing related work and had no easy way to see it.

That’s why we built [Kepler](https://svrgn.substack.com/p/introducing-kepler-inflections-home). We linked market research, technology notes, and meeting notes so someone researching AI agents could find the team’s earlier work on infrastructure and developer tools. When the partners published a thesis, they could go back to the research behind it instead of piecing together months of conversations.

That research also paid off before a deal came in. If you already know the market, you can spend the first founder meeting testing what’s different about this company.

## From research to an investment thesis

A deal usually starts with a company. A thesis can start months earlier with a question like “what are counter-UAV systems still bad at?” Two founders might give you conflicting answers. Keep both conversations with the research so the next person can see where the team disagrees and what evidence would change its view.

Writing the thesis for founders forces a partner to make the argument explicit. It also gives founders working on that problem a reason to reach out. Being able to get from a draft back to the conversation behind a claim matters more here than having a polished company profile.

## What to build around

Start with a market the team is already researching. Can a partner pick up a colleague’s work, see the evidence for a thesis, and trace it back to the original conversation? A CRM can tell you who met a founder. Research about the underlying technology needs somewhere to live even before there’s a company to attach it to.

Here’s what I’d want in a research tool.

- **Links between ideas.** A note on one technology can belong to several lines of research. Let researchers connect it in place.
- **Search across the team’s work.** Surface relevant notes while someone is researching the next company. Knowing the exact title shouldn’t be a prerequisite.
- **A path to publication.** Give partners a way to assemble notes into a draft and keep the sources with it.

## When to build your own

For someone working mostly alone, I’d start with [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/). The files are Markdown, you can link ideas freely, and you can take the research elsewhere. [Roam](https://roamresearch.com/) and [Anytype](https://anytype.io/) are other options. If several partners edit drafts together, start with the collaborative tool they already use. At many funds that’s Notion.

A collaborative editor solves a different problem. If three people are editing the same memo, a shared draft in Notion may be exactly what you need. The friction comes when an associate has to know which memo contains a piece of research before they can find it again.

Pay attention to what's frustrating. Can't find connections you know exist? Publishing workflow is too manual? Research isn't connecting to sourcing? These pain points tell you what to build. When you do build custom, you'll know exactly what features matter because you've felt their absence.

### What justifies a custom build

Say a founder updates you on a counter-UAV test you discussed six months ago. Open the original interview. Can you see which market notes and thesis drafts cite it? Those links back to the interview are called backlinks. If your editor already shows them, the partner can find the drafts that need updating right there.

Building takes significant time. Three to six months for something genuinely useful. Longer to build something that supports your full research-to-publishing workflow. Make sure research and thesis development are actually core to your strategy before committing engineering resources.

The key technical challenges are more about product design than technology. How do you model relationships between research entities? What makes relevant connections surface naturally? How do you build publishing workflows that feel smooth? How do you ensure that the research experience is as good as your competition: Claude, ChatGPT, and Perplexity?

At Inflection, research and publishing were central to how the fund found companies. That justified maintaining a Next.js and Postgres application around the way partners worked. If most of your deals come through introductions and the team rarely revisits its notes, I’d put that engineering time into the CRM.
